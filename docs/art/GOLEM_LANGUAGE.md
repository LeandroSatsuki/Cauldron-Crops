# Cauldron Crops — Golem Language

## Purpose

This document defines the visual and conceptual identity of the golems of Cauldron Crops.

It exists to prevent future asset generation from drifting toward generic RPG golems, humanoid mascots, or "small versions of adult monsters".

Human approval remains the final authority. This document records the direction reached through visual iteration and is a design reference, not automatic approval of every future golem asset.

## Core Identity

> **Golems are physical manifestations of the world's magic formed through matter.**

A golem is not a humanoid creature wearing the appearance of a material.

The material determines its body, silhouette, extensions, weight, color, texture and behavior.

Conceptually:

**magic + matter → creature**

not:

**humanoid creature + material texture → golem**

## Age and Personality

Golems are naturally **young, lively and ageless**.

They are not miniature adults and do not need an adult form.

They should evoke the feeling of a small forest spirit: strange and unfamiliar at first, but increasingly endearing as the player observes its behavior.

They may be ancient in lore without visually appearing old.

### Desired emotional progression

The intended first impression is:

> "What is that?"

followed by:

> "It is moving strangely..."

and eventually:

> "Oh. It's actually kind of adorable."

The cuteness should emerge from **contrast and behavior**, not from conventional kawaii design.

### Avoid

- baby/toddler appearance;
- elderly appearance;
- warrior personality;
- boss/guardian presence;
- imposing elemental-monster design;
- permanent exaggerated smiles;
- oversized kawaii eyes;
- human facial expressions as the primary personality mechanism.

## Approved Direction: Moss Golem

The current strongest visual reference is the approved moss-golem direction developed during PixelLab iteration.

Its conceptual structure is:

**central stone-like mass**
→ **moss grows over the material**
→ **living roots emerge from the body**
→ **roots become functional appendages**
→ **small magical creature**

The creature should feel as though a stone naturally developed roots and became alive through magic.

The moss-and-stone body is compact and small, approximately 60–80 cm in-world.

The final successful direction uses brown root material consistently from the body through the vine-like extremities.

## Anatomy

Golems do not have a universal humanoid anatomy.

Their functional anatomy emerges from their material.

A central material mass may become the body. Roots, branches, fibers, fragments, crystals, clay or other structures may become limbs and functional extensions.

### Silhouette test

A golem should remain recognizable and interesting when reduced to a silhouette.

If removing the face makes the creature stop working, the design is relying too heavily on facial features.

### Material test

The creature should still feel born from its material when facial details are ignored.

## Anti-Pattern: Obvious Anatomy

A material-based golem must NOT be designed by first constructing a humanoid skeleton and then replacing the body parts with material.

Avoid an obvious:

**head → neck → torso → arms → hands → legs**

hierarchy.

Avoid designs where branches, roots, crystals or other material structures merely occupy the positions of human limbs.

The creature should read as one organically formed organism before the viewer can identify individual anatomical parts.

If the viewer can immediately point to "the head", "the arm", "the leg" and "the torso", reconsider the design.

The material should create the anatomy, not decorate a pre-existing anatomy.

## Extremities

Extremities must remain consistent with the material.

For the moss golem:

**root → branching root → vine-like tapered tips**

The successful direction does NOT use human hands.

Preferred characteristics:

- 2–4 major tapered tips where appropriate;
- irregular lengths;
- slight natural curls;
- flexible vine/root appearance;
- enough functionality to grasp, pull, carry and interact with objects.

Avoid:

- palms;
- wrists;
- knuckles;
- five-finger hands;
- human finger anatomy;
- clawed humanoid hands.

## Facial Design

The face is secondary.

A small luminous eye or subtle pair of magical points can create personality, but the creature should not depend on a human-like face.

The successful moss-golem direction demonstrates that a simple eye combined with strange roots, an asymmetrical body and slightly awkward implied movement can create the desired contrast.

Avoid:

- large cartoon eyes;
- human mouths;
- human facial anatomy;
- exaggerated kawaii expressions.

## Movement and Behavioral Read

The visual design should imply movement that is:

- gentle;
- curious;
- slightly awkward;
- lively;
- organic;
- materially plausible.

The golem should look capable of helping around the farm without looking like a miniature humanoid worker.

## Material Determines Species

Future golems should not be created by recoloring the moss golem.

Each material must answer:

> **If the world's magic gave this material life, what kind of creature would naturally emerge from it?**

Examples:

- Moss + stone → rooted, vegetative, mineral organism.
- Wood → should derive its structure from branches, knots, bark and fibers.
- Crystal → should derive its body from facets, fragments, internal light and crystalline growth.
- Clay → should derive its form from soft masses, cracks and molded material.

These are conceptual examples, not final designs.

## Magic and the Inner Spirit

Every golem must carry a visible but restrained **vestige of magic**.

The magic is not a decorative effect added on top of the creature. It represents the magical spirit inhabiting and animating the material.

The visual form of this vestige may change according to the material, but it should always provide evidence that the creature is more than animated matter.

For the moss-and-stone golem, this appears as **small cracks with subtle blue magical light inside the stone body**.

The spirit may manifest in other materials through:

- light visible inside cracks;
- an inner glow beneath translucent or layered material;
- magical energy visible between structural fragments;
- faint luminous growth;
- tiny internal particles;
- another material-appropriate manifestation.

The exact color does not have to be blue for every species. The important rule is the **presence of an internal magical life-sign**, not a universal effect color.

### Subtlety rule

The magical spirit should normally be **discovered, not announced**.

At first glance, the viewer should primarily perceive the material and the creature.

Only on closer inspection should the viewer notice evidence that something magical is alive inside it.

Avoid making the spirit the most visually dominant feature.

Avoid:

- large glowing cores;
- exposed magical hearts;
- large luminous cracks;
- bright internal channels;
- obvious magical symbols;
- large external auras;
- heavy magical smoke;
- excessive particles;
- magic that overwhelms the material.

## Visual Language

Target:

- refined contemporary pixel art;
- organic silhouettes;
- controlled asymmetry;
- material-driven forms;
- moderate detail;
- readable small-scale silhouettes;
- rich but controlled palette;
- strange first, charming second.

Avoid:

- generic RPG golems;
- humanoid stone men;
- armor-like material shells;
- generic mascot anatomy;
- heavy black outlines;
- excessive neon;
- excessive particles;
- 8-bit aesthetics;
- classic 16-bit RPG character styling;
- photorealism;
- 3D-rendered appearance.

## Production Rule

When generating a new golem:

1. Define the material.
2. Define what physical process could plausibly make that material become alive.
3. Derive the body from that process.
4. Derive the functional appendages from the material.
5. Establish the creature's youthful/ageless personality.
6. Give the creature a restrained, material-appropriate visible vestige of its inner magic.
7. Check silhouette without facial details.
8. Check material identity without facial details.
9. Check whether the anatomy reads as an organic whole rather than a humanoid skeleton.
10. Check whether the magic is discovered rather than announced.
11. Only then add restrained magical accents or surface details.
12. Preserve the shared species philosophy without forcing shared anatomy.

## Reference Status

### Approved conceptual direction

**Moss Golem — current reference**
- small;
- youthful/ageless;
- stone-and-moss core;
- living brown roots;
- vine-like root extremities;
- subtle internal blue magical light visible through cracks;
- simple luminous eye;
- strange but gentle;
- slightly awkward implied movement.

This is the current visual north for the species.

It is not a mandate that every future golem share the same body.

## Key Rules

> **Golems should be strange before they are cute. Their cuteness should emerge from the contrast between an unfamiliar magical body and gentle, curious, slightly awkward behavior.**

> **Every golem must show some restrained evidence of the magical spirit inhabiting its material. The spirit should normally be discovered, not announced.**

> **No two golems need to share the same anatomy. They share a philosophy, not a body plan.**

> **Never start from a humanoid skeleton. Let the material create the creature.**
